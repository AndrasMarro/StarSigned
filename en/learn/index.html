How the zodiac works

Where the twelve signs come from, how your sign is calculated, and the surprising astronomy behind it.

A short history

  1. c. 1000 BCE

    Babylonian astronomers record the stars the Sun, Moon and planets pass through. The tablet series MUL.APIN lists these “constellations in the path of the Moon”.

  2. 5th century BCE

    Babylon divides the Sun’s path into twelve equal parts of 30 degrees each. This is the birth of the twelve signs, separate from the uneven constellations.

  3. c. 130 BCE

    The Greek astronomer Hipparchus discovers precession: the slow wobble of Earth’s axis that shifts the equinoxes against the stars.

  4. 2nd century CE

    Claudius Ptolemy writes the Tetrabiblos in Alexandria. It becomes the foundation of Western astrology for more than 1,500 years.

  5. 8th–12th century

    Scholars in Baghdad and across the Islamic world translate, preserve and extend Greek astronomy. Many star names we use today, like Aldebaran, are Arabic.

  6. 17th century

    With Kepler, Galileo and Newton, astronomy becomes a physical science and the two fields go separate ways.

  7. 1930

    The London Sunday Express prints a horoscope for the newborn Princess Margaret. Its popularity leads to the sun sign columns found in newspapers ever since.

How your sign is calculated

01

The ecliptic

Over a year, the Sun appears to travel once around the sky along a line called the ecliptic. The zodiac is a band around this line.

02

Twelve equal slices

The circle of 360 degrees is cut into twelve signs of 30 degrees each. The Sun moves almost one degree per day, so it spends about a month in each sign.

03

Starting point

The tropical zodiac, used here and in most Western astrology, starts at the spring equinox, around 20 March. That moment is 0 degrees Aries.

04

Your sun sign

Your sign is the 30-degree slice the Sun was in when you were born. Because the calendar and the solar year do not line up perfectly, the change-over can shift by a day from year to year, hence cusp birthdays.

05

Decans

Each sign is split into three decans of 10 degrees. The first is ruled by the sign itself, the next two by the following signs of the same element.

06

More than the Sun

A full birth chart also places the Moon, the planets and the rising sign (ascendant). For those you need your exact time and place of birth.

Signs are not constellations

The signs carry the names of constellations, but they are not the same thing. Earth’s axis wobbles like a spinning top, a cycle of about 25,800 years. Since the signs were defined, the equinox has drifted roughly 24 degrees, almost a whole sign, so the Sun now stands in a different constellation than its sign on most days. Sidereal (Vedic) astrology follows the stars instead, which is why your sign there is usually the previous one. The constellations also have very different sizes, and the Sun crosses a thirteenth: Ophiuchus, the serpent bearer.

How this site calculates

Sun sign
Standard tropical date ranges. Births within one day of a change-over are flagged as a cusp.
Moon phase
Days since a known new moon (6 January 2000), divided by the average lunar month of 29.53 days.
Moon sign
A short formula for the Moon’s position on the ecliptic. Accurate to about 1 to 2 degrees, so it can be off for a few hours around a sign change.
Sidereal sign
Your birthday moved back by about 24 days, the current gap between the tropical and sidereal zodiac (Lahiri).
Constellation
The dates when the Sun crosses the official constellation borders set by the International Astronomical Union in 1930.
Life path
All digits of your birth date added up and reduced to one digit, keeping the master numbers 11, 22 and 33.
Daily readings
Picked from a pool of sentences with a random generator seeded by the date and sign. Same day, same reading for everyone. For fun, not prediction.

Quick facts

  • “Zodiac” comes from the Greek zōidiakòs kýklos, the “circle of little animals”.
  • “Horoscope” means “hour watcher” in Greek. It originally referred to the rising sign, which changes every two hours.
  • Weekdays are named after the seven classical planets: Sunday (Sun), Monday (Moon), and in French mardi (Mars), mercredi (Mercury), jeudi (Jupiter), vendredi (Venus), and Saturday (Saturn).
  • The planet glyphs are old symbols too: ♀ for Venus is a hand mirror, ♂ for Mars a shield and spear.
  • Uranus, Neptune and Pluto were only discovered in 1781, 1846 and 1930. Before that, Saturn, Jupiter and Mars ruled Aquarius, Pisces and Scorpio.
  • The Sun spends about 45 days in the constellation Virgo, but less than ten in Scorpius.
